# Brand Identity Checklist for Founders: 12 Elements Every Startup Needs

You can build a brand for years and still lose customers to a competitor who simply looks more consistent. Brand identity is the visible and verbal system that makes your business instantly recognisable. Use this 12-point checklist to see what you have, what's missing and what to fix first.

## What is brand identity?

Brand identity is the collection of design and communication elements that represent your business: name, logo, colours, typography, imagery, voice and the rules for using them. Brand *image* is what customers think. Brand *identity* is what you control.

## The 12-point brand identity checklist

### 1. Brand name
- Easy to spell, say and remember
- Domain and social handles available
- Trademark status checked
- No unfortunate meaning in other languages you sell in

### 2. Positioning statement
One sentence describing who you serve, what you offer and why you're different. Everything else should align with it.

### 3. Brand story and values
A short, honest account of why you exist and what you stand for. This fuels your About page, pitch deck and packaging copy.

### 4. Logo suite
Don't stop at one logo file. A complete set includes:
- Primary logo
- Horizontal and stacked versions
- Icon or monogram for small spaces (favicon, app icon, profile picture)
- Light and dark background versions
- Black and white versions

### 5. Colour palette
- 1 to 2 primary colours
- 2 to 3 supporting colours
- Neutrals for text and backgrounds
- HEX, RGB and CMYK values noted for screen and print

Choose colours for contrast and readability, not just beauty.

### 6. Typography
- A headline font
- A body font
- Rules for sizes and weights
- Web-safe or licensed fonts so your website matches your print work

### 7. Imagery and photography style
Decide whether you use bright lifestyle shots, minimal product photography, illustrations or real customer photos. Write down lighting, colour treatment and composition rules.

### 8. Iconography and graphic elements
Patterns, shapes and icon styles that make your brand recognisable even without the logo.

### 9. Tone of voice
Define 3 to 4 personality traits and show examples. For instance: *"Confident, not arrogant. Friendly, not casual."* Add do's and don'ts for captions, emails and ads.

### 10. Core templates
Ready-made layouts save time and keep you consistent:
- Social media posts and stories
- Presentation deck
- Email signature and letterhead
- Invoices and proposals
- Product label or packaging dieline (if applicable)

### 11. Brand guidelines
A short document that brings it all together: logo usage, colours, fonts, imagery, voice and examples of correct and incorrect use. Even 10 pages is enough for a startup.

### 12. Asset library
Store final files in one shared folder with clear names: logos (SVG, PNG), fonts, templates, photography and brand guidelines. Your team and vendors should never have to ask "where's the latest logo?"

## Quick self-audit: score your brand

Give yourself one point for each "yes":

- Can a stranger describe what we do after 5 seconds on our site?
- Do our website, social profiles and packaging look like one brand?
- Is our logo clear at small sizes (favicon, WhatsApp profile)?
- Do we use the same colours and fonts everywhere?
- Do we have a written tone of voice?
- Can someone new use our brand without asking us questions?

**5 to 6:** Strong foundation. Focus on growth.
**3 to 4:** Gaps in consistency. Tighten the system.
**0 to 2:** Time to rebuild the foundation before spending on ads.

## Common brand identity mistakes

- **Designing a logo before positioning.** Pretty but pointless.
- **Too many fonts and colours.** Simpler is more memorable.
- **Ignoring small-size usage.** Many customers will see your logo as a tiny thumbnail on a phone or marketplace.
- **No guidelines.** Every designer starts from scratch and the brand drifts.
- **Trend-chasing.** A style that's popular this year may look dated by next year.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the main elements of brand identity?
Name, logo, colour palette, typography, imagery style, voice and brand guidelines are the core elements.

### What is the difference between a logo and brand identity?
A logo is one part of brand identity. Identity is the complete system of visuals and language that makes your brand consistent and recognisable.

### How many colours should a brand use?
Most brands work best with one or two primary colours, a few supporting colours and neutrals.

### Do startups need brand guidelines?
Yes. Even simple guidelines prevent inconsistency as soon as a second person starts creating content for you.

### When should I refresh my brand identity?
When your audience, product or positioning has changed meaningfully, or when your current look no longer reflects the quality of your business.
