# Startup Website Checklist: 15 Things Founders Need Before Launch

Your website is often the first and only chance to convince a stranger. Visitors decide in seconds whether you look credible, relevant and worth contacting. A slow, confusing or generic site doesn't just look bad. It quietly loses leads every day.

Use this 15-point checklist before you launch (or relaunch) your startup website.

## Part 1: Message and strategy

### 1. A clear headline that says what you do and for whom
Visitors should understand your offer in five seconds. Avoid vague lines like "Innovating the future." Say what you do, who it's for and the main benefit.

**Weak:** "Smart solutions for modern businesses."
**Strong:** "Cold-brew coffee delivered fresh to Bangalore offices, every Monday."

### 2. One primary goal per page
Decide what you want visitors to do: book a call, buy, request a quote, sign up. Every page should push toward one main action.

### 3. A visible call to action above the fold
Buttons such as "Get a Quote," "Book a Demo" or "Shop Now" should be visible without scrolling and repeated down the page.

### 4. Social proof
Add testimonials, client logos, case studies, ratings or media mentions. Real names and specifics build far more trust than generic praise.

### 5. A strong About page
Founders buy from founders. Share your story, team, mission and the problem you solve. Include faces and real names.

## Part 2: Design and experience

### 6. Mobile-first design
Most Indian users browse on phones. Test every page on small screens: readable text, thumb-friendly buttons, no horizontal scrolling.

### 7. Fast loading speed
Slow pages lose visitors and hurt search rankings. Compress images, avoid heavy scripts, use modern formats and reliable hosting. Test with Google PageSpeed Insights.

### 8. Consistent branding
Colours, fonts, logo and tone should match your packaging, social media and marketplace listings. Inconsistency makes a young brand feel unreliable.

### 9. Simple navigation
Keep the menu short: Home, Services or Products, About, Portfolio or Case Studies, Contact. Make the contact path obvious.

### 10. Easy contact options
Offer a short form, email, phone and WhatsApp. Remove friction. Every extra field reduces submissions.

## Part 3: SEO and technical basics

### 11. SEO-friendly structure
- One H1 per page with your main keyword naturally included
- Descriptive page titles and meta descriptions
- Clean URLs (e.g., /branding-services not /page?id=23)
- Logical headings (H2, H3)
- Descriptive alt text on images

For a deeper plan, read our [SEO guide for startups](/blog/seo-for-startups-india.html).

### 12. Analytics and tracking
Install Google Analytics 4 and Google Search Console before launch. Set up conversion tracking for form submissions, calls and purchases so you know what works.

### 13. Security and trust signals
Use HTTPS, display a privacy policy, add terms where needed and keep software updated.

### 14. Local presence (if you serve a city or region)
Add your city name naturally in content, create a Google Business Profile and keep name, address and phone consistent across the web.

### 15. A plan for ongoing content
A website is not a one-time project. Plan to add blog posts, case studies and updates regularly. Fresh, helpful content builds authority and brings in organic traffic.

## Pre-launch quality check

Before you go live, test:

- Every link and button works
- Forms deliver emails to the right inbox
- Pages look right on Android, iPhone and desktop
- Images are compressed and have alt text
- Titles and meta descriptions are written for every page
- Thank-you pages and tracking are working
- 404 page is friendly and helpful

## Common website mistakes startups make

**Building a website before the message is ready.** Design can't fix unclear copy.

**Using a template with no customisation.** It looks like everyone else's and doesn't reflect your brand.

**Hiding the price or the next step.** If people can't tell how to proceed, they leave.

**Ignoring SEO until months later.** Structure is easiest to get right at the start.

**Too many pages too soon.** A focused five-to-eight page site often beats a sprawling one.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many pages does a startup website need?
Most startups can launch with 5 to 8 pages: Home, About, Services or Products, Case Studies or Portfolio, Blog, Contact, and legal pages.

### How long does it take to build a startup website?
Simple sites can take a few weeks. Larger sites with custom design, content and integrations take longer. Content readiness often decides the timeline.

### Is a website still necessary if I have social media?
Yes. A website is the one place you own. It builds credibility, supports SEO and connects your ads, social profiles and marketplaces.

### How can I make my website rank on Google?
Target clear keywords, write helpful content, keep the site fast and mobile-friendly, and earn quality links over time. Our [digital marketing and SEO services](/digital-marketing.html) help with this.

### What is the best platform for a startup website?
It depends on your needs. WordPress, Webflow, Shopify and custom builds all work. Pick based on content needs, e-commerce requirements and long-term flexibility.
