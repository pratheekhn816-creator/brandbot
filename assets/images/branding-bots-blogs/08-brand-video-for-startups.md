# Brand Video for Startups: What to Shoot First, What to Skip, and How to Make It Convert

Video is no longer a "nice to have." Customers watch product demos before buying, scroll Reels before following and judge your credibility by how you show up on camera. For a startup, a few well-planned videos can do the job of dozens of static posts.

But video can also burn budget fast. Here's how to decide what to shoot first and how to get real value from every frame.

## Why video matters for startups

- **It explains faster.** A 30-second demo can replace paragraphs of text.
- **It builds trust.** Seeing the founder, team, process or product in use makes the brand feel real.
- **It works everywhere.** The same footage can feed your website, Instagram, YouTube, WhatsApp, ads and marketplace listings.
- **It improves conversion.** Product pages and listings with clear video often help customers decide.

## Types of brand videos (and when to use them)

### 1. Brand film or "About us" video
Tells your story, mission and values in 60 to 120 seconds. Best for your homepage and investor or partner conversations.

### 2. Product or explainer video
Shows what the product does, how it works and why it's better. Ideal for product pages, Amazon listings and ads.

### 3. Social media videos and Reels
Short, vertical clips designed for discovery and engagement. Think behind-the-scenes, tips, product moments, trends adapted to your brand.

### 4. Testimonial and case-study videos
Real customers describing their experience. Strong for B2B, services and high-consideration products.

### 5. Ad creatives
Short, direct videos built to drive clicks and sales on Meta, YouTube or marketplaces. They need strong hooks and clear calls to action.

### 6. Founder and team videos
Personal updates, origin stories or expert explainers that humanise the brand and build authority.

## What to shoot first

If your budget is limited, prioritise in this order:

1. **A 30 to 60 second product or brand video** for your website and ads
2. **A bank of short clips** (product close-ups, packaging, process, usage) to edit into social content for weeks
3. **One customer or founder story** for trust
4. **Marketplace video** if you sell on Amazon or similar channels

Shoot more than you need in one session. It costs far less to capture extra footage on the day than to organise another shoot.

## How to plan a video that actually works

### Define the goal
Awareness, leads, sales or trust? One video, one main goal.

### Know the viewer
Decide who you're speaking to and where they'll see it. A website hero video differs from an Instagram Reel.

### Write a simple script and shot list
Even a one-page outline helps:

- **Hook (first 3 seconds):** a visual or line that stops the scroll
- **Message:** one clear idea
- **Proof:** demo, result or testimonial
- **Call to action:** what should the viewer do next?

### Plan the logistics
Location, props, product samples, talent, permissions, timing, music licensing and backup plan.

### Design for sound-off viewing
Many people watch muted. Use captions and clear visuals.

### Create multiple formats
Plan your shoot so you can export 16:9 for websites and YouTube, 9:16 for Reels and Shorts, and 1:1 or 4:5 for feeds.

## Quality: what matters most

You don't need a Hollywood budget, but you do need to get the basics right:

- **Clean audio.** Poor sound loses viewers faster than imperfect visuals.
- **Good lighting.** Soft, even light makes products and people look better.
- **Stable shots.** Use tripods or gimbals.
- **Consistent branding.** Colours, fonts, logo placement and tone should match your identity.
- **Tight editing.** Cut anything that doesn't earn its place.

## Where to use your videos

- Website homepage and product pages
- Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn and YouTube
- Amazon and Flipkart listings
- Paid ads
- Email campaigns and WhatsApp broadcasts
- Sales decks and investor presentations
- Store displays and events

Repurposing is the key to getting more value from each shoot.

## Common video mistakes

- Starting with a long logo animation instead of a hook
- Trying to say everything in one video
- Ignoring mobile-first framing
- Using unlicensed music
- No clear call to action
- Posting inconsistently or abandoning the channel after a few uploads
- Measuring only views instead of clicks, enquiries and sales

## How to measure video performance

Track what matters to your goal:

- **Awareness:** reach, views, watch time
- **Engagement:** shares, saves, comments
- **Consideration:** website visits, product page views
- **Conversion:** enquiries, add-to-carts, purchases

Test different hooks and formats, double down on what works and drop what doesn't.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a brand video be?
For websites, 60 to 90 seconds is a good starting point. For social media, 15 to 45 seconds often performs better. Keep it as short as the message allows.

### How much does video production cost for a startup?
Costs depend on the length, locations, crew, equipment, talent and editing complexity. Ask for an itemised quote and plan one shoot that produces multiple assets.

### Do I need to appear on camera as a founder?
Not always, but founder-led content often builds trust quickly, especially for new brands.

### What equipment do I need for DIY videos?
A modern smartphone, tripod, basic lighting and a lapel microphone can produce good results for simple content.

### How often should a startup post video?
Choose a rhythm you can sustain, such as a few times a week, and focus on quality and consistency over volume.
